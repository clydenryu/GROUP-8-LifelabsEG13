4. Research Findings (Problem & Solution) (Mathew Xian Go)

The Problem

Many mission critical institutions like (students, business owners, and others including -- banking backends, government databases) rely on obsolete software and hardware because replacing them is expensive and risky. However, this creates massive security blind spots / vulnerabilities, heavy maintenance costs, and integration roadblocks with modern cloud tech.

Our Solution

Incremental Modernization.

For decades, old legacy systems remain widely used across core industries because complete replacements are costly and risky. However, relying on this obsolete tech creates a critical “Legacy Trap,” exposing organizations to unpatched security vulnerabilities, unexpected hardware failures, and compatibility roadblocks with modern cloud applications. Rather than attempting high risk overhaul, the most effective solution is Incremental Modernization.

By utilizing API wrapping (a software technique used to modernize outdated legacy systems without having to completely rewrite their underlying code) to bridge old databases with modern interfaces, refactoring outdated code, and implementing phased cloud migrations, organizations can secure their operations and restore agility without disrupting day to day functionality.

SOFTWARE HEALTH CHECKUP

What is a legacy system?

A legacy system is old software or hardware that is still being used even though newer options exist. Schools, banks, and small businesses often keep using them because replacing them costs money and takes time.

Warning signs your software is outdated

  • Frequent freezing or crashing: the program stops responding or closes by itself.
  • No recent updates: the developer no longer fixes bugs or adds features.
  • No security updates: known weaknesses stay open for hackers to use.
  • Compatibility problems: it doesn't work well with newer devices, files, or apps.

Take the 4-question checkup

What to do if your software is outdated

  • Check for updates and install them if they're available.
  • Back up your files before changing or upgrading anything.
  • Ask your IT team or teacher to check the system's security.
  • Plan an upgrade to modern software, step by step instead of all at once.

This checkup is a simple guide, not a professional security audit.
